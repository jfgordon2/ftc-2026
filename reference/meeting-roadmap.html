Trace, Evaluate, Rebuild meeting roadmap

Coordinate the exact 16-meeting sequence with readiness gates, source and evidence ownership, robot access, and honest fallback work. A fallback preserves learning and records blockers; it never converts missing hardware or an unperformed test into a pass.

Practice now, build as we go

We start with last season’s fully assembled REV and goBILDA StarterBots. Each keeps its own program, configuration, and test records. We will build a BIOBUZZ competition robot with a required launcher while the practice robots support learning.

Meetings 4-13 reserve 35 minutes of each three-hour meeting for physical build work. Keep one powered station under the coach’s supervision. A practice pass does not qualify a different robot; repeat affected checks when moving to the new build.

Robot selection, build milestones, and transfer checklist

Source, decision, and measurement expectations

F (documented fact): name the source and what it covers. Vendor examples apply only to their matching named assembly; current Competition Manual, Team Update, and Q&A control current game and event claims. D (team decision): record the selected behavior, criteria, or limit and why the evidence supports it. M (team measurement): record robot/configuration setup, procedure, trial ID, units, result, and date. Unperformed measurements remain pending delivery evidence.

Readiness gates

  • Meeting 1 setup gate: identify the installed manufacturer and exact revision, active hardware configuration, Control Hub, Driver Station, and matching source path before treating any vendor assumption as applicable.
  • Meeting 2 motor gate: secure the robot on stable support in the game arena, clear every wheel, verify one exact saved name and motor at a time, and prove explicit zero and Stop. No floor travel occurs.
  • Meeting 3 camera gate: preserve M2 source/configuration and control notes; verify webcam and printed tag scale; test camera telemetry, missing target, and Stop/restart at one supervised stationary station.
  • Meetings 6-8 TeleOp: the robot in use drives and ROBOT in StarterRobot.java matches it. Meeting 7 also needs the webcam and the mentor’s motion and feed switches. Autonomous work for a match waits until the TeleOp routine has passed, or the team has written down what it switched off.
  • Meetings 9-12 Autonomous: Hub direction and drive direction are checked on the robot in use before any program drives by itself. A route is run empty three times before shooting is switched on. The one-action autonomous from Meeting 9 always stays as the backup.
  • Meeting 13 PIDF: write down the starting P and F first. Without a reliable launcher, measure and graph only.
  • Meetings 14-16 event readiness: the event robot, a timer, Self-Inspect all green, and a backup for every drive-team job. After Meeting 15 the code changes only with a full practice match afterwards.
