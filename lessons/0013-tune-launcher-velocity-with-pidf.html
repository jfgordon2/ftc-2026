Today’s goal

Leave knowing whether the launcher’s F and P should stay as they are or change, with numbers on paper to show why.

The launcher does not just get “power”. A small controller in the Hub keeps adjusting the motor to hold the speed we ask for. Two numbers steer it. F is its first guess at how hard to push for a given speed. P is how strongly it corrects when the speed is wrong. Today you measure what the current numbers do, try one change at a time, and keep a change only if the measurements are better.

Start here

  1. Measure the launcher as it is. Baseline steps.
  2. Try F, one step at a time. F steps.
  3. Try P, one step at a time. P steps.
  4. Keep or put back. Save your work.

One robot runs at a time. Students who are not at the robot read the code for the next step, fill in predictions, or work on the build. Swap jobs after every test.

Before power

  • Everyone stands behind the robot when the launcher can spin. Shoot at a safe target.
  • If the wheel sounds rough, speeds up and down by itself, or will not settle, let go of the bumper and press B to go back to the starting numbers.
  • One student drives. A different student keeps a hand near Stop on the Driver Station and may press it at any time without asking.
  • Press Stop and wait for everything to stop moving before you touch, load, measure or pick up the robot.
  • Clear a jam only with the robot switched off.
Stop first: if the robot goes the wrong way, something is loose, a ball jams, or the numbers on the screen look wrong, press Stop. Then work out why.

Steps and worksheets

Words you need

Target speed: the speed we ask for. Measured speed: what the encoder reports. Error: target minus measured. F (feedforward): the controller’s first guess at the push needed for a speed. P (proportional): extra push in proportion to the error. Recover: get back to the target speed after a ball slows the wheel.

Measure the launcher as it is

Program: Tune Launcher PIDF. It changes F and P only while it is running. Nothing is saved until you type numbers into the code.

Try F

  1. Wheel stopped. Press D-pad UP once. Run three times and fill in the “F up” rows.
  2. Press B. Press D-pad DOWN once. Run three times and fill in the “F down” rows.
  3. Compare the averages with the baseline. Is one of them clearly better by the rule you agreed?
  4. Keep the best F on the screen for the next part. If nothing was clearly better, keep the starting F.

What to look for: with F too low the wheel is slow to reach speed. With F too high it shoots past the target before settling.

Try P, then decide

  1. With your chosen F, press D-pad RIGHT once. Three runs. Fill in the “P up” rows.
  2. Go back one step, then press D-pad LEFT once. Three runs. Fill in the “P down” rows.
  3. What to look for: higher P recovers faster after a shot, but too high makes the speed bounce above and below the target.
  4. Draw a bar graph of seconds to recover for baseline, P up and P down.
  5. Set the P and F you think are best and do three final runs.
  6. Decide: are the final runs better than the baseline by the rule you agreed at the start? A small difference that changes from run to run is not better.

Save your work

  • The starting P and F are on the worksheet so they can be put back.
  • If the final numbers were clearly better, they are typed into LAUNCHER_P and LAUNCHER_F in StarterRobot.java. If not, the code is unchanged, and that is a fine result.
  • If the numbers changed, one shooting mark was re-checked with Tune Shooter, because the speed table was measured with the old numbers.
  • The code builds. Commit it with a short message, for example “Meeting 13 launcher F and P”.

Meeting 14 practices finding and fixing faults quickly.

Mentor preparation

  1. The tuner uses the robot’s normal launcher speed and starts from the P and F the Hub is using. One press changes F by 0.5 and P by a tenth of its starting value. The REV practice robot has no P and F in our code, so its starting numbers are the Hub’s built-in ones.
  2. Use a fresh battery and swap it halfway. A tired battery changes the results more than a small change in P.
  3. Leave I and D at zero. The lesson does not use them.
  4. If there is no reliable baseline, for example the launcher is still being built, students measure and graph the baseline only and change nothing.
