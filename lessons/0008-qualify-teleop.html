What you will prove

The team will run one unchanged, complete qualification routine three consecutive times. Each run must pass safe initialization, safe defaults, drivetrain, every installed action still classified required, simultaneous operator work, release, reverse or recovery, telemetry, and Stop without a code-related failure or safety violation.

Qualification proves only the exact robot, configuration, code identity, control-map revision, setup, and required scope recorded today. One successful action, a vendor demonstration, or three partial runs do not qualify TeleOp.

Team choice M08-D01 Required parity means that every capability in the matching StarterBot path which Meeting 4 classified required and verified as installed appears in the routine and passes. Optional or genuinely unavailable actions do not count against parity, but a required action may not disappear silently.

Words you need today

Qualification
An objective decision that all recorded acceptance criteria passed for one exact scope and identity.
Parity
Coverage of every matching, installed capability that the approved Meeting 4 map still classifies required.
Complete routine
One uninterrupted pass through every qualification item, including reset, initialization, all required actions, Stop, and inspection.
Consecutive
One after another with no failed routine or change to code, configuration, hardware, setup, scope, card, or criteria between credited runs.
Defect
Observed behavior that fails an already-approved requirement or acceptance criterion.
Feature request
A new behavior, preference, or improvement outside the approved qualification criteria.
Reclassification
A formal approved change from required to optional or unavailable after evidence shows a feature cannot be made safe.
Recovery identity
The exact tested commit and tag, or another exact team-approved version identifier, used to retrieve the qualified state.

Before you touch the robot

  • Name the Driver Station operator, Stop owner, driver, mechanism operator, observer, recorder, programmer, and reset/mechanic duties. One student may hold two nonconflicting duties, but Stop ownership remains explicit.
  • Use the integrated Meeting 7 TeleOp, signed Meeting 4 control map, current configuration, installed-hardware record, fault log, and each action's acceptance evidence.
  • Press Stop, switch off the Hub, disconnect the battery, and release stored energy before inspection, adjustment, reset, loading, or contact.
  • Freeze the bounded game-arena setup, accepted battery condition, load, gamepad assignments, control-map revision, routine revision, criteria, code identity, and configuration before the first credited attempt.
  • Do not begin powered qualification while any enabled behavior has an unresolved safety-critical defect.
Team choice M08-D02 Powered-work safety policy: the Stop owner ends the routine for unexpected motion, communication loss, wrong ownership, looseness, battery movement, wire contact, collision, binding, hard-stop contact, stall, heat, smell, unusual sound, field intrusion, or any failed safety criterion. Press Stop, switch off, disconnect, call stopped, and preserve the evidence. An aborted routine does not count, and no inspection, adjustment, reset, loading, or contact begins before that shutdown.

Follow along: turn the control map into a gate

  1. Fix the scope. Copy every Meeting 4 action into the qualification record with its map-row ID, classification, installed evidence, and current enabled/disabled state. Why: parity cannot be judged from memory. Success: every map row is accounted for and every required installed action is enabled and listed for testing.
  2. Check current game relevance. Confirm each retained required action against the current game overview, rules, Team Updates, and applicable Q&A; vendor capability alone does not establish relevance or legality. Success: the record names the current source revision and any blocked interpretation. Source M08-F04
  3. Write observable criteria. For initialization, defaults, drive, each required action, simultaneous work, release, reverse/recovery, telemetry, and Stop, write the request, expected output or state, visible result, safe result, abort condition, and evidence field. Success: another pair can call Pass or Fail without asking what the criterion means.
  4. Separate facts from physical proof. Motor and servo API documentation defines command behavior, but only testing this installed robot can qualify physical behavior. Source M08-F03 Success: API facts cite the pinned class while physical criteria remain pending until observed.
  5. Freeze identity and setup. Record robot identity, active configuration, OpMode, code/recovery candidate identity, map and routine revisions, battery condition, arena setup, loads, and role plan. Success: the first credited attempt cannot begin while any identity is unknown or inconsistent.
  6. Rehearse once without credit. Complete every item, then classify each observation as defect, feature request, or no issue. Correct only qualification defects through Meeting 7's evidence-first process; queue requests. Success: the full routine passes once and no unresolved enabled safety defect remains before the count begins.

Prepare qualification with your partner

Three pairs: rotate every 20 minutes through all three workstreams. Two pairs: one pair combines Scope and Record while the other combines Robot and Routine; switch after 30 minutes. Swap reader/recorder and driver/navigator responsibilities at the rotation.

1. Scope and source audit

  1. Compare the matching StarterBot capability trace, installed robot, and every Meeting 4 row.
  2. List every matching, installed, required capability; list optional and unavailable rows separately with their approved rationale.
  3. Confirm each required row has release, reverse/recovery, simultaneous-work, telemetry, and acceptance criteria.
  4. Block any missing row, unexplained exclusion, unmatched vendor assumption, or stale game-rule decision.

Success looks like: the qualification scope provides required parity without claiming that vendor capability proves team qualification.

2. Robot and routine readiness

  1. Inspect battery retention, wiring, connectors, fasteners, wheels, mechanisms, clearances, loads, and the bounded game arena while power is disconnected.
  2. Rehearse exact startup, neutral controls, initialization, Start, action order, simultaneous scenarios, release, recovery, Stop, shutdown, inspection, and reset.
  3. Verify explicit zero or measured safe hold at initialization, no request, release, blocked conflict, and Stop wherever applicable.
  4. Name the Stop owner and control owner at every live step; allow no physical reset duty during live control.

Success looks like: the team can execute one complete routine without inventing a step, value, or role.

3. Identity and record readiness

  1. Record the exact tested source/recovery candidate, active configuration, selected OpMode, robot, map revision, routine revision, setup, and evidence locations.
  2. Prepare separate defect and feature-request queues. A request cannot change the frozen routine during credited runs.
  3. Prepare the append-only attempt log and numbered continuation sheets. Every attempt needs its own chronological row with every criterion, actual observation, student, checker, Stop/release observation, reset trigger, and resulting streak.
  4. Prepare the final field for an exact existing commit/tag or other recovery identifier. Qualification records an identifier established through the team's version-control process; this lesson does not direct creation of one.

Success looks like: every run can be tied to one unchanged tested state and every observation has one destination.

Test it safely: three consecutive complete routines

One complete routine

  1. Reset and identify: with the robot stopped, off, and disconnected, restore the recorded start and load; inspect readiness; confirm code/recovery candidate, robot, configuration, OpMode, map, routine, battery condition, setup, criteria, and roles. Pass: every frozen identity agrees.
  2. Initialize safely: connect and switch on through the approved sequence, establish Driver Station communication, select the recorded OpMode and configuration, keep controls neutral, and press INIT. Pass: no exception or unexpected motion occurs; every output is explicit zero or its measured safe hold; telemetry identifies the expected state.
  3. Test drivetrain: after the clear call and Start, perform the map's required drive, steer, align, slow/control mode, and commanded stop cases. Pass: directions, limits, release, telemetry, and safe neutral behavior match the card.
  4. Test every still-required installed action: execute each required map row at its qualified load and within its measured bounds, including required feedback, interlocks, and telemetry. Pass: every row meets its own observable acceptance evidence; no required row is skipped or substituted.
  5. Test simultaneous operator work: run each approved concurrent drive/mechanism or mechanism/mechanism scenario and each conflict policy from the map. Pass: ownership, priorities, blocked requests, feedback, and commanded outputs match the approved design.
  6. Test release and reverse/recovery: release each active input and execute each required reverse, clear, or recovery case. Pass: every affected output returns to explicit zero or measured safe hold, retained states remain only when approved, and telemetry exposes the resulting state.
  7. Stop and inspect: the Stop owner presses Stop from the routine's defined active case; the team verifies documented Stop behavior, switches off, disconnects, calls stopped, and inspects the robot. Pass: outputs and holds match the verified Stop criteria, no safety violation or code-related failure occurred, and actual observations are recorded.

Count rule

Team choice M08-D03 Powered qualification and reset policy: qualification requires one frozen state to produce three complete passing attempts in adjacent chronological log rows with streak values 1, 2, and 3. Preserve every failure and abort. Any reset trigger invalidates the earlier streak; refreeze the state and let the next complete pass begin a new streak at 1.

  1. Record streak 1 only when the entire routine passes. Reset the robot and rotate roles only while it is stopped, switched off, and disconnected.
  2. For streak 2, repeat the same complete routine, including identity, initialization, every required action, simultaneous work, release/recovery, Stop, inspection, and reset.
  3. For streak 3, repeat that unchanged complete routine again. Three partial routines, nonconsecutive passes, or action-by-action passes do not qualify TeleOp.
  4. Any failed criterion, abort, code-related failure, safety violation, or change to code/recovery candidate, configuration, hardware, setup, scope, map, routine, criteria, or enabled behavior resets the consecutive count to zero. Preserve the attempt row, diagnose, correct, refreeze, and let the next complete pass begin a new streak at 1.

Formally revise unsafe scope

Team choice M08-D04 Reclassification and nonwaivable policy: a failed required feature is a defect, not permission to lower the gate. Repair and retest it first. Safe initialization, drivetrain control, Stop, and every action that remains required are nonwaivable. If evidence shows that another required action cannot be made safe for the current event, complete every step below.

  1. Record the action/map-row ID, failed criterion, trial and fault-log evidence, attempted correction and retest, and why the feature cannot be made safe.
  2. Propose a new classification of optional or unavailable. Record the safety rationale, event-strategy cost, and affected operator plan; do not delete the original row.
  3. Obtain named mentor approval and named team approval with date, then issue a new Meeting 4 map revision.
  4. Disable the behavior in code, verify that no input can command it, remove it from the event plan and driver instructions, and list it explicitly as excluded on the qualification record.
  5. Freeze the revised scope, reset the streak to zero, and repeat the complete qualification process. No routine credited under the old scope carries forward.
Nonwaivable capabilities: safe initialization, drivetrain control, Stop, and every action still classified required must pass. They cannot be waived, substituted, or reclassified merely to claim qualification. Repair and retest them; otherwise TeleOp is not qualified.

If the robot, required mechanism, or live test is unavailable

  1. Complete the map-to-routine audit, current-game check, identity record, objective criteria, role rehearsal, defect/request forms, and reclassification form without power.
  2. Use saved logs, telemetry, videos, configuration records, and Meeting 7 evidence to rehearse Pass/Fail decisions. Label every conclusion artifact review only.
  3. If only one mechanism is unavailable, do not substitute telemetry, a fixture, or a different mechanism for required physical parity. Record that action as blocked unless the full formal reclassification process is actually approved and completed.
  4. If the full robot is unavailable, run fixed input-decision-output traces and a complete spoken routine, but record TeleOp not physically qualified. Three paper or simulated routines do not satisfy M08-M01.
  5. Record the missing resource, prerequisites, exact physical routine still required, owner, and target date. Preserve the integrated TeleOp and prior known-good identity for recovery.

Success looks like: useful preparation and honest blockers, with no qualification, parity, safety, or recovery claim beyond the evidence.

Each student explains

Without partner help, choose one required action and trace its map row through request, decision/state, commanded output, physical result, simultaneous case, release, recovery, telemetry, and Stop criterion. Then explain one event that resets the three-run count and why a commit or tag alone does not prove qualification.

Record each student's evidence

  • Record safe operation only when the student performs assigned live steps, callouts, release, Stop, shutdown, and evidence handoff correctly.
  • Record tracing only when the student connects one required map row to all of its observable qualification criteria.
  • Record diagnosis only when the student separates a defect from a request and names the evidence and retest.
  • Record scope reasoning only when the student can state every reclassification step and all nonwaivable capabilities.
  • Record each student separately. Team qualification does not prove individual independence.

Mentor preparation

  • Bring the signed Meeting 4 map, matching StarterBot trace, installed-hardware record, integrated Meeting 7 TeleOp, active configuration, fault records, exact candidate identity, and current game-source revision.
  • Audit that every matching, installed, required capability appears in the routine. Do not replace the map with a generic seven-action checklist or vendor demonstration.

Gate enforcement: a failed or changed routine resets the streak to zero. Safe initialization, drivetrain, Stop, and still-required actions are nonwaivable. A feature exclusion is valid only after evidence, optional/unavailable rationale, mentor and team approval, a revised map, code disablement, event-plan removal, explicit qualification exclusion, and a complete restart with the next passing attempt recorded as streak 1.
