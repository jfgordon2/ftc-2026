Compose the Autonomous Route

Use current game constraints and the team's measured drive and turn actions to design a driving-only route, prove each segment first, and keep the exact Meeting 10 sequence ready as the lower-risk fallback.

    180 minutes
  • Team
    4-6 students, 2-3 pairs
  • New idea
    Students own the route decision
  • Team work
    Rules, route, code, field, and evidence

What your team will produce

A student-composed, driving-only Autonomous candidate that traces to current game and starting constraints, calls only measured Meeting 10 drive and turn methods, and has evidence for every segment before any complete-route trial. The exact Meeting 10 sequence remains unchanged, identified, and selectable as fallback.

Words you need today

Constraint
A current rule, start condition, field boundary, period limit, or robot limitation that the route must obey.
Objective
The driving-only result the team chooses before writing the route.
Segment
One named drive or turn action with a recorded start, stop condition, expected result, and safe cleanup.
Frozen setup
The exact robot, code, configuration, field references, start pose, battery acceptance, surface, mechanism posture, and reset method held unchanged for comparable trials.
Error
The measured difference between the intended and actual endpoint or heading, with named references and units.
Acceptance criteria
The pass conditions the team approves before seeing complete-route results.
Fallback
The exact lower-risk Meeting 10 sequence, code identity, configuration, and evidence that remain unchanged and selectable.

Before you touch the robot

  • Name the Driver Station operator, Stop owner, field/reset lead, observer/measurer, programmer, and recorder.
  • Press Stop, switch off the Hub, and disconnect the battery before robot or field contact.
  • Locate and independently verify the exact Meeting 10 fallback commit, file/class, Driver Station display name, configuration, route sequence, and evidence before editing.
  • Create a separately named candidate. Never edit, rename, overwrite, retag, or repurpose the fallback.
  • Keep mechanisms in their documented safe travel posture. This meeting adds no mechanism action.
  • Walk every proposed segment with power disconnected and inspect wheel, frame, bumper, battery, wire, field-element, and exclusion-zone clearance.
Unexpected direction, speed, route, target behavior, continued motion, timeout, field entry, interference, communication loss, looseness, battery movement, wire contact, binding, sound, heat, or odor means press Stop. Verify safe output and no motion, say stopped, switch off, and disconnect before entry or contact.

Students choose the objective

  1. List at least two objectives that can be attempted using driving only. Do not include launch, intake, servo, sensor-triggered, or other mechanism actions.
  2. For each objective, cite the current source sections, identify its required start and endpoint, and list route, collision, timing, and evidence risks.
  3. Reject any objective that needs an unmeasured action, unresolved rule interpretation, unsafe clearance, or unavailable field setup.
  4. Choose the smallest useful objective the measured methods can support. Record why it is preferred and who approved it.
  5. Before complete-route tests, define pass/fail, endpoint and heading tolerances, route/time limit, required valid-trial count, and the acceptance rule. These are team decisions, not FIRST rules.

Compose named, measured actions

Source M11-F02 Pinned SDK v12.0 RobotAutoDriveByEncoder_Linear demonstrates repeated parameterized encoder-action calls. Use that high-level structure only. Team method behavior and every distance, angle, output, tolerance, timeout, conversion, and direction must come from the exact Meeting 10 evidence, not sample constants.

  1. Audit the inputs: name the exact Meeting 10 drive and turn method revision, conversion record, configuration, test row IDs, supported range, known error, and escape conditions. A missing or failed input blocks that action.
  2. Make one segment card per action: record a student-chosen name, physical intent, method call and measured parameters, start fixture, expected endpoint/heading, active guard, feedback/target condition, timeout or escape, human abort, explicit zero, and motor-mode cleanup.
  3. Order only eligible cards: write the route as readable calls in execution order. A student who did not write it must be able to narrate the physical route from the calls alone.
  4. Check transitions: the expected end of one segment must match the next segment's expected start. Mark clearance at each transition.
  5. Build and inspect: verify the candidate has a distinct file/class/display name and that the diff changes no Meeting 10 fallback file, identity, configuration record, or evidence.

Test one segment at a time, then the route

Use this complete run cycle for every segment and route trial

  1. With Hub off and battery disconnected, restore the recorded fixture and inspect robot, field, route, battery, wires, and mechanism-safe posture.
  2. Connect the accepted battery, switch on, verify Hub and Driver Station status, deploy when required, and select the exact candidate identity.
  3. Press INIT. Verify configuration identity, safe outputs, no motion, telemetry, measurement tools, roles, and all stop conditions.
  4. The operator says robot live; the observer confirms the route clear; then the operator presses Start.
  5. Observe active, feedback/target, timeout/escape, and abort conditions. Any unexpected behavior means Stop.
  6. Require explicit zero and documented mode cleanup on completion or abort. Press Stop, verify no motion and safe output, and say stopped.
  7. Switch off, verify Hub off, disconnect, then measure and record start identity, endpoint/heading, error with units, elapsed time, result, exit reason, stop proof, student, and checker.

Gate every segment before integration

  1. Test Segment 1 alone from its recorded fixture. Repeat to the predeclared segment criterion without changing code or setup.
  2. Only after Segment 1 passes, test Segment 2 alone from its own recorded fixture. Continue in route order.
  3. If a segment fails, keep the row. Change no more than one declared variable, predict the effect from row evidence, build, inspect the diff, repeat readiness, and retest that segment. Do not test later segments or the full route until it passes.
  4. A safety correction ends the current comparison. Record it, re-freeze the setup and candidate, and begin that segment's evidence again.
  5. Integrate only when every segment has qualifying row IDs and every transition has passed the unpowered clearance check.

Keep the Meeting 10 fallback exact and recoverable

The fallback is the tested Meeting 10 sequence: 36 inches forward, 12 inches backward, and 90 degrees left using the exact measured methods and code identity supported by its records. Those distances and angle identify that fallback; they are not values for the student-designed route.

If powered testing is unavailable

  1. Keep the route candidate disabled and preserve the exact Meeting 10 fallback.
  2. Complete the current-source constraint record and student objective decision, clearly marking unresolved rules questions.
  3. Compose named calls only from Meeting 10 evidence and trace every start, guard, stop, explicit zero, and cleanup path.
  4. Use a robot outline or inert chassis to create and repeat the start/segment fixtures and perform transition-clearance checks.
  5. Label all outcomes unpowered planning - not M11-M01 robot evidence. Record the blocked gate, owner, required physical test, and date.

Mentor preparation

  • At delivery, open the current English Competition Manual, latest Team Update, and relevant Q&A; record revision/access dates and prepare the exact applicable section/rule links.
  • Audit Meeting 10's measured drive/turn methods, supported ranges, physical rows, frozen fixtures, known limits, and exact guided-sequence fallback identity.
  • Prepare current field references and measurement tools, but do not choose the objective, route, parameters, tolerances, trial count, or acceptance rule for students.
  • Supply no new power, time, distance, angle, encoder, geometry, tolerance, mechanism, or conversion value. Missing evidence blocks the action.
  • Require segment evidence before integration and preserve every valid failure. Do not use full-route repetition to diagnose an unqualified segment.
Integration gate: current-source constraint record, approved driving-only objective, exact measured action evidence, named segment cards, every stop/safe-output/cleanup path, frozen setup, unpowered reset and clearance checks, candidate build/diff, exact fallback recovery, and assigned roles must all pass before powered testing.
