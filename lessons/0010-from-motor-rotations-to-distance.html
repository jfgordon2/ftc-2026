Today’s goal

Leave with a ticks-per-inch number measured on this robot, and a drive and a turn that land where you asked.

The drive motors count their own rotation in ticks. The code turns inches into ticks with one number, TICKS_PER_INCH. Today you calculate that number from the motor and the wheel, measure it on the floor, and keep whichever the tape measure supports. Meeting 5 did a quick version of this on the practice robot; do it again on any robot whose wheels or motors are different.

Steps and worksheets

Words you need

Encoder: the sensor in a motor that counts how far it has turned. Tick: one count from the encoder. Circumference: the distance around the wheel, which is how far the robot rolls in one wheel turn. IMU: the sensor in the Control Hub that tells which way the robot faces.

Calculate ticks per inch

Both goBILDA StarterBots use drive motors that count 537.7 ticks for one turn of the wheel and wheels 96 mm across. The REV practice robot counts 420 ticks per wheel turn, and REV’s code treats its wheel as 3 inches (76.2 mm) across. Where 537.7 and 420 come from: the encoder sits on the motor and counts 28 ticks per motor turn, and the gearbox turns the motor 19.2 times (goBILDA) or 15 times (REV) for each turn of the wheel. 28 × 19.2 is about 537.7; 28 × 15 = 420. Check the numbers for your robot before you trust them.

  1. Distance for one wheel turn = wheel diameter × 3.1416. In millimetres: ________
  2. Change to inches: divide by 25.4. ________ inches per wheel turn
  3. Ticks per inch = ticks per wheel turn ÷ inches per wheel turn. ________
  4. How many ticks should a 24-inch drive be? ________

Compare your answer with the starting value on your robot’s TICKS_PER_INCH_… line near the top of robot/Drive.java (for the BIOBUZZ robot, TICKS_PER_INCH_BIOBUZZ_GOBILDA). The comment under those lines shows the same calculation. They should agree to within 0.1.

The calculation assumes the wheel never slips and is exactly 96 mm. Real wheels squash and slip a little. That is why we measure.

Measure straight drives

Program: Tune Drive.

  1. Pointer on the start mark. INIT. Press D-pad UP for 24 inches forward. Start.
  2. When it stops, write the left and right ticks and the seconds from the screen. Press Stop. Measure the real distance.
  3. Do it three times forward, carrying the robot back to the mark each time. Then once backward with D-pad DOWN.
  4. For each run: measured ticks per inch = average of left and right ticks ÷ measured inches.
  5. Average your forward runs. If the average differs from the code by more than 0.5, replace the number on your robot’s TICKS_PER_INCH_… line with yours, build, and run once more to check.
  6. Work out the robot’s speed: inches ÷ seconds. You need this in Meeting 11.

Check turns

Turns do not use ticks. The robot turns until the heading sensor in the Control Hub (the IMU) says it has turned far enough.

  1. Tape a square corner on the floor. Line the robot up along one side.
  2. INIT. Turn the robot a little to the right by hand: the heading number should go up. Put it back.
  3. Press D-pad RIGHT for 90 degrees right. Start. Compare the robot with the taped corner.
  4. Repeat for D-pad LEFT.
  5. If a turn is off by more than about 3 degrees, or the robot wobbles back and forth at the end, write that down for the mentor. The turn gain is in Drive.java.

Save your work

  • Your measured ticks per inch is in Drive.java, with the runs behind it on the worksheet.
  • The robot’s speed in inches per second is written on the worksheet.
  • The code builds. Commit it with a short message, for example “Meeting 10 measured ticks per inch”.
