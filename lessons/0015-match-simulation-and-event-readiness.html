Practice a full match workflow on the BIOBUZZ robot and prepare the event kit.

Start here

A simulation includes the handoffs and reset, not just the robot moving.

  1. Check the current official match procedures and inspection checklist. Use the actual competition robot and launcher.
  2. Assign drivers, operator, Stop owner, observer, reset duties, and backups. Walk through the routine without power.
  3. Run setup, INIT, autonomous, transition, TeleOp, Stop, and reset. Use official timing and game constraints.
  4. Complete at least three full simulations. Record failures and retest the complete routine after a correction.
  5. Save the exact event version, pack the recovery kit, and make sure each critical role has a practiced backup.

Robot for today: Use the exact launcher being studied; final event checks use the BIOBUZZ robot. See the practice and build plan.

Build task: Use inspection and recovery blocks to fix event blockers. Pack batteries, cables, tools, and labeled spares.

Keep a short result: robot + code version; what we changed; expected result; observed result; next step. Use the detailed worksheet when the test calls for measurements.

Read the safety checklist before power. Open the detailed steps for code, test cases, and worksheets; the mentor helps choose the section needed next.

Before you touch the robot

  • Begin from the Meeting 14 identity, inspection record, recovery guide, accepted battery list, unresolved blockers, and exact event robot. Stop if any identity is missing.
  • Name the Driver Station operator, driver, mechanism operator when used, independent Stop owner, observer/recorder, pit responder, reset lead, and timekeeper. Name a different practiced backup for each responsibility.
  • Before contact, loading, repair, or reset: press Stop; verify zero commanded output and no motion; say stopped; switch the Hub off; disconnect and isolate the battery; and release or support stored energy.
  • Inspect battery condition and retention, structure, fasteners and witness marks, wheels, guards, mechanisms, starting posture, robot sign, wiring, connectors, strain relief, cable travel, and every current checklist item. Quarantine damaged or uncertain parts.

Travel-test game-arena gate

Team choice M15-D02 This travel-test safety contract is a BIOBUZZ team decision for bounded robot motion, not a FIRST game rule. Record every item before motion; any blank, failed, or uncertain item blocks the test.

  • Game arena and marks: use a stable, level, dry game arena with visible boundaries. Secure field elements and starting guides; record every missing or substitute element; and record the marked start, route, and stop.
  • Secured system: verify the robot frame, retained battery, mounted Hub, routed wires, every attached piece, each mechanism, and the test artifact are secured for the planned motion. Confirm wiring cannot enter wheels or mechanisms.
  • Exclusion area: mark and clear the route, full mechanism reach, stopping distance, safety margin, and reset path of people, tools, bags, cords, chairs, loose objects, and overhead or side obstructions. Keep everyone outside this exclusion area during motion.
  • Named control roles: record the Driver Station operator and a different independent Stop owner. Give both clear sightlines, stable footing, no cable trip hazard, and immediate unobstructed access to Stop.
  • Control state: confirm controls are neutral, gamepads are assigned, and the exact configuration and OpModes are selected.
  • Independent signoff: after all other items are recorded, a second student who did not perform the original checks must independently verify every pre-motion check and sign and date the travel-test record before motion. Any missing verification or signature blocks motion.
  • Identify a safe approach and exit route. Nobody enters for field reset until Stop, zero output, no motion, stopped, Hub off, and battery disconnection are all confirmed.
Full Stop triggers: the Stop owner ends the run for unexpected motion; a person or loose object entering the exclusion area; the robot or test artifact crossing the marked route or stop; wrong program, configuration, gamepad assignment, or controls; loss of sightline, communication, or control; repeated reset; movement or loosening of the frame, battery, Hub, wire, attached piece, mechanism, field element, or test artifact; collision, binding, hard-stop contact, or stall; abnormal sound, heat, swelling, smoke, or odor; any failed safety criterion; or any safety doubt. Preserve the attempt. Never reproduce a dangerous symptom.

Detailed steps, code, and worksheets

What you will make the team able to do

Run the exact event robot through setup, INIT, Autonomous, transition, TeleOp, Stop, powered-down reset, role handoff, and evidence-based recovery. Complete at least three full simulations before the team may freeze one exact event candidate. Record a primary and practiced backup for every responsibility and every event-critical artifact.

Team choice M15-D01 BIOBUZZ uses at least three full simulations, no mid-run coaching, written freeze criteria, and a team-approved event tag name. These are team reliability decisions, not FIRST rules.

Measure it M15-M01 Status: pending delivery evidence. The lesson prepares the record; only completed simulations on the identified robot can supply the result.

Words you need today

Match simulation
One uninterrupted practice of the complete current workflow from setup through powered-down reset and handoff.
INIT
The Driver Station action that prepares the selected program before Start. Student code may already be running, so safe outputs must be checked.
Autonomous
The match period when the selected Autonomous program controls the robot without gamepad driving.
Transition
The current official change from Autonomous to the driver-controlled period, including every required Driver Station and drive-team action.
TeleOp
The team's driver-controlled program and period.
Mid-run coaching
A prompt about setup, controls, strategy, code, or recovery after a simulation begins. A safety Stop call is never coaching.
Event blocker
A safety, legality, inspection, setup, control, required-behavior, recovery, or identity failure that prevents event release.
Late feature
A new improvement that is not needed to correct an event blocker. It is recorded and deferred during freeze work.
Event candidate
One exact combination of source, deployed app, robot configuration, OpModes, hardware, controls, and test records proposed for event use.
Freeze
The team decision to stop adding features and protect the exact passing event candidate.
Rollback
A separately named, known-good identity the team can restore if the event candidate cannot be used.

Follow along: trace one complete workflow

  1. Setup: use the current manual, latest Team Update, applicable Q&A, local instructions, and arena map to place the identified robot and controls. Why: remembered event steps can be stale. Success: the observer can name the source version/check time and every setup item passes.
  2. INIT: connect the accepted battery while the Hub is off; switch on; confirm Robot Controller/Driver Station connection, gamepad assignment, robot configuration, event-candidate identity, and exact Autonomous and TeleOp; then press INIT. Success: telemetry identifies the expected state and outputs remain zero or at their measured safe hold.
  3. Autonomous: after the current official start cue, run the selected Autonomous without gamepad driving or prompts. Success: the observer records the intended route/actions, guards, telemetry, and actual result.
  4. Transition: follow the current official transition and Driver Station actions. If using automatic loading or Practice Timer, first compare it with the current manual and local procedure. Success: the exact TeleOp is selected and initialized at the proper cue without coaching.
  5. TeleOp: drivers perform only the approved event plan and required recovery controls. Success: controls, safe releases, simultaneous work, telemetry, and robot behavior match the tested card.
  6. Stop: at the official end cue or any Stop condition, press Stop and verify zero commanded output and no motion. Say stopped. Success: Stop behavior and the exact end time are recorded.
  7. Reset: switch the Hub off, disconnect/isolate the battery, release stored energy, inspect, and only then enter the arena. Restore the recorded starting state without hiding damage or changing the candidate. Success: the observer signs the normal powered-down reset.
  8. Role handoff: the outgoing student gives the incoming student the source/cue card, exact identity, arena state, controls, Stop conditions, defects, and evidence location. Success: the incoming student repeats the information before taking responsibility.
  9. Failure recovery: preserve the symptom, time, identity, field state, messages, telemetry, and logs. Use the Meeting 14 guide to test one explanation and make only the smallest event-blocking fix. Success: the fault record names the cause, change, original retest, complete simulation retest, owner, and result.

Practice roles and handoffs with your partner

Three pairs: one pair prepares event procedure and roles, one prepares robot/arena/inspection, and one prepares identity/log/recovery. Rotate every 13 minutes so every pair completes all three work areas. Two pairs: combine procedure with identity and combine robot with observation; switch after 20 minutes. At each rotation, swap the student doing the check with the student reading and recording it.

1. Procedure and people

  1. Turn current setup, period, transition, Stop, and reset instructions into a short cue card with source version and check time.
  2. Assign every live responsibility and a different practiced backup. Mark combinations that current drive-team rules do not permit.
  3. Rehearse one complete spoken flow and two handoffs. Do not use remembered timing as authority.

Success looks like: every person can state the next cue, Stop condition, primary, backup, and source without hidden mentor knowledge.

2. Robot, arena, and inspection

  1. Complete the travel-test game-arena gate, current inspection material, and today's Driver Station and Robot Controller Self-Inspect.
  2. Record every unavailable field interaction or checklist item as blocked. Do not infer full-field, collision, clearance, scoring, or inspection proof.
  3. Prepare accepted batteries, exact tested spares, tools, charging flow, and safe reset marks.

Success looks like: the robot and arena pass written checks, or the exact blocker, owner, and required check are visible.

3. Identity, observation, and recovery

  1. Read the full source commit, saved tested diff identity if any, build/deploy record, app identity, configuration, Autonomous, fallback Autonomous, TeleOp, robot hardware revision, battery, gamepads, map/criteria revisions, and test-record IDs aloud.
  2. Prepare an append-only attempt log. The observer records without prompting and preserves every failed, coached, stopped, and passing attempt.
  3. Verify the rollback identity and restore instructions from Meeting 14. Locate one practiced backup copy of each event-critical file or device.

Success looks like: another student can identify, observe, restore, and verify the exact state without guessing.

Swap live roles after every powered-down reset

The Driver Station operator controls program selection and Stop on the Driver Station. The driver and mechanism operator use only their assigned gamepads. The independent Stop owner watches the robot and arena with uninterrupted Stop access. The observer/recorder gives no prompts. The pit responder acts only after shutdown. The reset lead enters only after the stopped state. The timekeeper supplies practice cues from the current cue card. Combine duties only when they do not conflict and current event rules permit it; never combine live driving or Driver Station operation with independent Stop ownership.

Four students: assign Driver Station/driver, independent Stop owner, observer/timekeeper, and pit/reset. Use one gamepad operator if the approved plan permits it. Rotate one position after each powered-down reset. Six students: assign Driver Station, driver, mechanism operator, independent Stop owner, observer/timekeeper, and pit/reset. Rotate one position after each powered-down reset. Before each run, write a primary and a different practiced backup for every active responsibility.

Test it safely: at least three full simulations

Team choice M15-D01 No mid-run coaching: after setup begins, mentors and inactive students do not prompt procedure, controls, strategy, code, or recovery. Anyone may call Stop for safety. A prompted run remains useful practice but is not a qualifying full simulation.
  1. Open the next row before setup. Record sequence number, date/time, source check, exact candidate identity, arena, battery/controllers, roles, backups, prediction, and Stop conditions.
  2. Run every phase. Complete setup, INIT, Autonomous, transition, TeleOp, Stop, shutdown, inspection, normal reset, and role handoff under the current cue card.
  3. Record the actual result. Mark each phase Pass, Fail, Abort, Coached, or Blocked and link messages, telemetry, logs, inspection, and Stop evidence. Never erase an attempt.
  4. Separate event blockers from late features. An event blocker must be fixed or leave release blocked. Put late features in the future-work list; do not add them to the candidate today.
  5. Recover after shutdown. Preserve evidence, test one explanation, and make one smallest event-blocking correction. A restart or disappearing symptom is not a fix.
  6. Retest completely after a blocker fix. Repeat the original failed check, every affected bounded safety/behavior check, and then one complete setup-through-handoff simulation on the corrected exact identity. No pass from before the fix can qualify the corrected candidate.
  7. Build the qualifying set. Complete at least three full, uncoached passing simulations on one unchanged exact identity. If any simulation fails, aborts, is coached, is blocked, has an identity mismatch, or requires an event-blocking change, preserve it and restart the qualifying count at 1 after correction and complete retest.

Measure it M15-M01 Status: pending delivery evidence. Add a chronological row before every attempt. Use continuation sheets when needed. Only actual complete robot simulations count.

Record the event-candidate decision

Source M15-F03 GitHub documents that a tag names a repository point. A tag does not contain robot configuration, hardware state, deployment proof, or physical test evidence and does not prove event readiness.

Team choice M15-D01 The team chooses and records the tag naming pattern. A suggested name such as event-YYYY-MM-DD is a BIOBUZZ option, not a GitHub or FIRST requirement. Create or move no tag until the team's authorized version-control workflow and freeze criteria permit it.

Freeze is blocked: the decision must be BLOCK if any DS app version, RC app version, device OS context, Hub firmware, compatibility result, compatibility source/version or URL, access/check date, or checker field is blank, failed, unknown, or uncertain. Freeze only when all team criteria pass, the installed-control-system row records a passing compatibility result and its source, access/check date, and checker, at least three full simulations pass on the same exact identity, no event blocker remains, and rollback and backups are proven. After freeze, any event-blocking code, configuration, hardware, setup, map, criteria, or enabled-behavior correction creates a new candidate and requires the original check, all affected checks, and at least three new full simulations before another freeze decision.

If the field, robot, network, or official source is unavailable

  1. Partial field: rehearse procedure, roles, cues, controls, Stop, shutdown, handoff, and reset. Record absent interactions; do not claim full-field path, collision, clearance, scoring, or simulation proof.
  2. No safe robot motion: use the actual identity records, Driver Station screens, prior logs/video with provenance, and cue cards. Do not count a physical simulation or freeze newly unproven behavior.
  3. No live official source: label the last saved material currency unverified. Do not supply remembered timing or rules. Assign a named live recheck before simulation, freeze, and event use.
  4. Inspection checklist still unavailable: use the current manual, live Event and Team Resources status, current Self-Inspect, and dated comparison material, but keep checklist completion blocked until FIRST publishes the current checklist.
  5. Not enough time or equipment: preserve the last proven identity and record the exact blocked simulation, missing resource, owner, and date. Preparation is useful but is not a passing M15-M01 result.
