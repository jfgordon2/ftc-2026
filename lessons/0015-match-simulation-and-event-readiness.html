Today’s goal

Leave with three full practice matches played without coaching, the code that played them saved as the event code, and the kit packed.

A match is more than driving. It is setting the robot on the field, picking the right programs, 30 seconds of autonomous, a short pause, two minutes of TeleOp, and clearing the field. Today everyone practices the whole thing, and every job gets a backup.

Start here

  1. Learn the match flow and the jobs. Match flow.
  2. Play three full matches. Match steps.
  3. Get ready for the event. Readiness list.
  4. Save the event code. Save your work.

Before power

  • During a practice match only the drive team is at the field. Everyone else watches from outside the taped area.
  • One student drives. A different student keeps a hand near Stop on the Driver Station and may press it at any time without asking.
  • Press Stop and wait for everything to stop moving before you touch, load, measure or pick up the robot.
  • Clear a jam only with the robot switched off.
Stop first: if the robot goes the wrong way, something is loose, a ball jams, or the numbers on the screen look wrong, press Stop. Then work out why.

A drive team is up to four people: up to three drivers, one human player, and one drive coach. Only one of them may be an adult.

Decide today who does each job and who the backup is. A backup is someone who has done the job in a practice match, not someone who has been told about it.

JobWhat they do
DriverDrives the robot.
Second driver, if the team uses oneRuns the mechanisms.
Drive coachWatches the clock and the field and calls the plan. May not touch the controls.
Human playerHandles scoring elements where the rules allow.
Pit crewBattery, quick repairs, and getting the robot to the field on time.

Play three full matches

  1. Use a match timer. The official FTC practice timer sounds are linked in the sources.
  2. Start from “Set up” every time, including carrying the robot in.
  3. No coaching from outside the drive team during the match. A safety Stop is always allowed.
  4. After each match the recorder fills in one row. Then reset the field.
  5. Match 3: every job is done by its backup.

After a fix that touches the code, play one more full match before trusting it.

Get ready for the event

  • Self-Inspect is all green on the Driver Station and the Robot Controller.
  • The robot fits the 18-inch starting size with everything attached.
  • The team has read the newest Team Update and the event’s own instructions.
  • The inspection checklist has been walked through once.
  • Packed and labelled: robot, Driver Station, both gamepads, at least two batteries, charger, laptop and cable, tools, spare parts, tape, printed tags, the troubleshooting guide.
  • Every job has a backup who did it in a practice match today.

From now on the event code changes only if something is broken, and any change is followed by a full practice match.

Save your work

  • The match sheet has a row for each practice match: what worked, what broke, what you changed.
  • Every drive-team job has a backup who has practiced it.
  • The code that played the practice matches is committed with a message such as “event code”.
  • The kit is packed and the readiness list is ticked.

Meeting 16 is the capstone: each student shows they can change, build, deploy and test the robot code alone.

Mentor preparation

  1. Check the game materials page for a Team Update newer than 03 and for Q&A answers that affect the team.
  2. Set up as much of a field as you can: the tape field at least, with the start line, target and parking box.
  3. Bring a match timer, the inspection checklist, and the packing boxes.
  4. Stay out of the matches. Step in for safety only.
