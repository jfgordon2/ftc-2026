Reliability and Failure Recovery

Reliability means the exact system repeats its required behavior; recovery means evidence leads the team back to that verified behavior. Repeat the team's qualified flows, diagnose realistic failures, repair one cause at a time, and prove restoration.

What your team will prove

Starting from the exact qualified TeleOp, Autonomous protected by active/bounded/safe conditions, and accepted launcher/PIDF records, the team will repeat complete routines, safely diagnose approved failure cases, restore a known-good identity (the exact accepted system state), and rerun every affected flow (the complete operational sequence the failure or repair could change). The event guide will contain only steps that students completed and timed.

Meeting 14 does not add a feature. It tests whether students can recover startup, held-control, interrupted-run, configuration, and harmless mechanism problems without guessing, compounding changes, or losing a working rollback.

Words you need today

Reliability routine
An unchanged, complete sequence whose actual results are recorded on repeated attempts.
Failure case
An actual symptom or a mentor-prepared harmless condition used to practice diagnosis and recovery.
Fault record
The time-ordered link among a symptom, hypothesis, evidence, confirmed cause, one repair, retest, and prevention step.
Likelihood
The team's evidence-based judgment of how often a failure could occur in the current event setup.
Consequence
The harm to safety, control, inspection, or event performance if the failure occurs.
Evidence strength
How directly repeated observations, logs, telemetry, inspection, or measurements support the suspected cause.
Affected flow
The complete operational sequence that could have changed because of the fault or repair, not only the step that failed.
Recovery time
Elapsed time from recognizing the symptom until the restored affected flow passes.
Residual risk
A known chance or consequence that remains after recovery and must be accepted, reduced, or kept blocked.
Rollback identity
The exact earlier code, configuration, controller mode, coefficients, hardware state, and evidence needed to reverse a change.
Known-good restoration
Returning every affected identity to its recorded accepted state and proving it with the complete affected-flow retest.
Guard
A condition that permits an action to continue only while the action remains active, bounded, and safe.

Before you touch the robot

  • Name the Driver Station operator and independent Stop owner, plus the observer, reset lead, code driver, code navigator, and fault recorder. The Stop owner may press Stop without permission.
  • Use a Supported test for powered checks that do not require travel: secure the frame on stable support in the game arena, with every wheel clear of the support, floor, people, wires, and loose objects.
  • Use a Travel test only when the affected flow requires travel: begin at a marked start in the game arena, follow a marked route to a marked stop, and keep everyone outside the exclusion area.
  • Secure the robot, battery, Hub, wires, attached pieces, mechanisms, and test artifact. Before every powered attempt, state the prediction, setup, command, Stop conditions, explicit zero or measured safe hold, and evidence to record.
  • Press Stop, verify no motion, switch off the Hub, and disconnect the battery before contact, inspection, adjustment, configuration-related physical checks, reset, or loading.
  • Students may consume only an actual fault or a mentor-prepared, documented, previously proven harmless case. Students never create, seed, improvise, or conceal a failure.
Do not create damage for practice. Never short or reverse power, use a damaged or swollen battery, alter a live connector, loosen a robot fastener, defeat retention, jam or restrain a mechanism, force a hard stop, cause a stall, create heat, or interfere with communication. Press Stop for unexpected motion, direction, speed, continued output, failed guard, implausible telemetry, loss of control, field entry, looseness, battery or wire movement, binding, vibration, sound, heat, smoke, odor, or inability to command zero. Then verify no motion, call stopped, switch off, disconnect, preserve evidence, and escalate.

Prepare recovery with your partner

Three pairs: rotate every 20 minutes through all three workstreams. Two pairs: combine Identity and Records while the other pair prepares Cases and Risk; switch after 30 minutes. At every rotation, swap code driver/code navigator and physical inspector/recorder. No powered failure practice occurs during these workstreams.

1. Identity and rollback audit

  1. Copy the TeleOp, Autonomous candidate/fallback, and launcher/PIDF fields from the upstream evidence named above.
  2. Independently locate each exact code/recovery identity without changing the current working copy.
  3. List configuration, mode, coefficients, hardware posture, field setup, and test records that source identity alone cannot restore.
  4. Block any rollback with a missing artifact, unknown configuration, unread coefficient/mode, or unproved physical state.

Success looks like: another pair can distinguish source retrieval from complete physical and operational restoration.

2. Cases and risk ranking

  1. For each authorized case, state exact symptom, baseline, harmless setup proof, expected evidence, Stop conditions, restore action, and complete affected flow.
  2. Rank consequence from safety/control blocker to performance inconvenience; rank likelihood only from actual history, inspection, or repeated observation.
  3. Record evidence strength separately so a frightening guess does not become a confirmed cause.
  4. Choose the highest-risk case that is both safe to rehearse and supported by evidence. Unsafe or weakly defined cases stay blocked.

Success looks like: priority is traceable to consequence, likelihood, and evidence rather than whoever speaks first.

3. Fault and retest design

  1. Prepare one fault record per case with timestamps, identities, symptom, first evidence, hypothesis, cause, and owner.
  2. Permit one cause-related repair before the identical symptom check. A second change requires a new record and prediction.
  3. Name every affected initialization, action, release, guard, cleanup, Stop, reset, and recovery step that must be rerun.
  4. Start the recovery clock at symptom recognition and stop it only when the complete affected flow passes or is declared blocked.

Success looks like: a quick local fix cannot pass while an affected startup, guard, shutdown, or rollback check remains untested.

Run one evidence-first recovery cycle

  1. Run official checks first: run current Driver Station Self-Inspect and preserve its exact displayed result. Retrieve logs through the current FTC procedure when needed. Record source access date and app/device context. Success: official results remain distinct from the later local drill. Source M14-F01
  2. Freeze and repeat the baseline: verify every required identity and run the complete eligible TeleOp routine, complete selected Autonomous route, and launcher action/rollback readback needed by today's selected case. Success: the pre-case record proves the exact starting state; a failed baseline blocks the prepared case.
  3. Authorize one case: the mentor signs its prior harmless proof, setup, expected symptom, abort conditions, restore action, and affected flow. The team records likelihood, consequence, evidence strength, and priority before seeing today's outcome. Success: students receive the condition without preparing or creating it.
  4. Prepare the powered setup: use Supported test unless travel is essential to the affected flow. With the Hub off and battery disconnected, secure and inspect the complete setup. Then connect the accepted battery with the Hub off, switch on, confirm status and identity, select the exact test, press INIT, and verify explicit zero or measured safe hold. Success: all identities, roles, boundaries, predictions, commands, and Stop conditions match the card.
  5. Observe and preserve: call robot live, receive clear, expose only the approved symptom, start the recovery clock, and capture the first message, log, telemetry, physical observation, and operator input. Success: no restart, edit, adjustment, or second action erases the first evidence.
  6. Stop before diagnosis: press Stop, verify zero/safe hold and no motion, call stopped, switch off, disconnect, and preserve the setup. Success: nobody touches or changes the robot while it is live.
  7. Test one hypothesis: classify the likely layer, write one cause that the evidence can support or reject, and perform the smallest safe check. Confirm the cause before repair. Success: the fault record connects observation to evidence rather than to a guess.
  8. Repair one cause: change one code, configuration, connection, physical state, or operator step through its approved procedure. Record the exact before/after identity. Success: no hidden cleanup or second repair is bundled into the result.
  9. Restore known-good identity: independently verify the required code/recovery identity, active configuration, controls, hardware posture, Autonomous candidate/fallback, launcher controller mode, accepted PIDF readback, and exact pre-change controller read-back P/I/D/F tuple with its code/configuration identity where affected. Success: every relevant field matches the baseline record before motion.
  10. Repeat the original symptom check: recreate the same bounded setup and input. Success: the original symptom is absent for the predicted reason; otherwise preserve the failure and return to one new hypothesis.
  11. Rerun the complete affected flow: repeat all affected startup, INIT/default, action, simultaneous-input, release, guard, explicit-zero/safe-hold, cleanup, Stop, reset, and rollback-selection steps. For launcher work include readiness, controlled action, recovery, named output zeros, controller mode and coefficient readback. Success: every required row passes on one restored identity.
  12. Close or block: stop the recovery clock only after the complete retest. Record recovery time, actual result, residual risk, prevention, student, checker, and whether the event guide may include the steps. Success: unresolved or untested risk remains visibly blocked.

If powered testing or an upstream flow is unavailable

  1. Record the missing robot, mechanism, configuration, identity, upstream evidence, source access, or arena setup. Do not call the flow reliable or recovered.
  2. Run Self-Inspect only when the current Driver Station is available and record its actual result. Use captured official screenshots only as labeled practice, not today's Self-Inspect evidence.
  3. Audit exact TeleOp, Autonomous, launcher/PIDF, and rollback identities; trace guards, zero/hold, cleanup, and Stop paths; and practice fault-record decisions with captured evidence.
  4. Use powered-off inspection or a harmless mentor-prepared training fixture. Do not convert a paper diagnosis, build, restored file, or code trace into a physical recovery claim.
  5. Leave guide rows blank unless a prior complete recovery record for the same exact identity remains valid. Hand off every blocked physical baseline, failure case, affected-flow retest, recovery time, and residual-risk decision with an owner and date.

Success looks like: useful preparation, exact blockers, and no reliability claim beyond the available evidence.

Record individual evidence

  • Record safe operation only when the student performs assigned callouts, Stop/shutdown, evidence preservation, and no-contact boundary correctly.
  • Record diagnosis only when the student preserves first evidence, tests one hypothesis, and does not bundle repairs.
  • Record recovery only when the student independently verifies known-good identity and completes the affected-flow retest.
  • Record risk reasoning only when likelihood, consequence, evidence strength, recovery time, and residual risk are each justified separately.

Mentor preparation

  • Audit Meeting 8 qualification, Meetings 9-12 Autonomous guards/candidate/fallback, Meeting 13 launcher/PIDF accepted and rollback identities, exact configuration, hardware posture, and all open fault records.
  • Prepare at most one harmless case per category. For each, record the exact baseline, prior proof of harmlessness, zero/safe-hold state, expected symptom, evidence, affected flow, restore action, abort conditions, and setup-removal check.
  • Students consume cases only. Prepare and remove each condition yourself while the robot is stopped, Hub off, battery disconnected, and students away from the setup. Do not disclose the cause before evidence collection.
  • Exclude all power faults, damaged parts, live connector changes, loose robot fasteners, radio interference, unexpected actuator mapping, collision, restraint, jam, forced hard stop, stall, heat, or unproved mechanism state.
  • Prepare stable support in the game arena, marked Travel test boundaries where needed, accepted batteries, exact identities, logs, current Driver Station Self-Inspect, current FTC Docs, exact-part service documents, fault forms, and independent timing.
  • Reject any guide step without a completed recovery row and complete affected-flow retest. Do not lower a criterion after seeing a result.
Enablement gate: exact eligible baseline, harmless-case record, powered setup, Driver Station operator, independent Stop owner, prediction, explicit zero or measured safe hold, abort list, first-evidence plan, one-change boundary, original symptom retest, complete affected-flow retest, known-good restoration fields, recovery clock, and residual-risk decision must all be ready before the case begins.
