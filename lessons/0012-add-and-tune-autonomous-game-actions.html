Today’s goal

Leave with an autonomous that shoots its preloads and still finishes its route, or with the dry-run route kept as the version to use.

The route from Meeting 11 already stops at a shooting spot. Today the robot shoots there. It only feeds when the tag is in view, it is aimed, the range is in our speed table, and the wheel is at speed. Your job is to make those checks pass every time and to find the feed time that empties the robot.

Start here

  1. Measure the speed table on this robot. Table steps.
  2. Shoot in autonomous. Shooting steps.
  3. Tune the feed time. Tuning steps.
  4. Save. Save your work.

One robot runs at a time. Students who are not at the robot read the code for the next step, fill in predictions, or work on the build. Swap jobs after every test.

Before power

  • Everyone stands behind the robot’s starting position for the whole run. Nobody is between the robot and the target.
  • Count balls in and balls out. A ball left in the robot can fire the next time the launcher spins.
  • One student drives. A different student keeps a hand near Stop on the Driver Station and may press it at any time without asking.
  • Press Stop and wait for everything to stop moving before you touch, load, measure or pick up the robot.
  • Clear a jam only with the robot switched off.
Stop first: if the robot goes the wrong way, something is loose, a ball jams, or the numbers on the screen look wrong, press Stop. Then work out why.

Steps and worksheets

Words you need

Preload: a ball the robot starts the match holding. At speed: the launcher wheel is within 50 ticks per second of the speed asked for. Feed time: how long the feeder runs for one burst. It is a time, not a count of balls.

Measure the speed table on this robot

The Meeting 5 table was for the practice robot. A different launcher, different balls or a moved camera needs its own table.

  1. Start on a fresh battery. Read the Battery line on the Driver Station during INIT and write the voltage down. Press Stop and INIT again now and then to re-check. If it says SWAP or CHARGE, change the battery and shoot one finished row again.
  2. Use Tune Shooter and the Meeting 5 shooting steps: five shots, count, change the speed one step.
  3. Find a speed that hits at least four of five at the spot where your route stops, then at one spot a little closer and one a little farther.
  4. Type the three rows into ShooterCalibration.java. The camera range at your shooting spot must be between the first row and the last.

Switch shooting on

The mentor sets FEED_ENABLED = true in StarterRobot.java. Your route must have an aimAndShoot() line where the robot should shoot.

  1. Load the robot with its preloads. Count them.
  2. Program: Autonomous Route. INIT, choose alliance, cell and Start A. Press X so Shooting says ON.
  3. Check the INIT screen: “Speed table problem” must be blank. Place the robot at the shooting spot once and check “Speed table covers this range” says true.
  4. Start. Watch the state on the screen: MOVE for each drive or turn, then AIM, SHOOT, and MOVE again as it turns back and carries on.
  5. Press Stop when it finishes. Count the balls that left and the balls that hit.
The screen saysWhat it means
Shot skipped: speed table has no rows around … inchesThe drive before the shot stopped outside your table. Change that drive or measure another row.
Shot skipped: tags not lined up in timeThe camera could not see or centre the tags within 3 seconds. Check the camera tilt and the cell you chose.
Shot skipped: FEED_ENABLED is falseThe mentor’s switch is still off.
Shot stopped: Launcher spin-up timeoutThe wheel did not reach the speed in 4 seconds. Check the battery and the speed you asked for.
Out of time at 28 secondsThe route is too long. Remove a step or shorten a drive.

Tune the feed time

The feeder is allowed to run for FEED_SECONDS in ShotTimer.java. It pauses whenever a ball slows the wheel and carries on when the wheel is back at speed, but the clock keeps running. Too short and balls are left in the robot. Too long and the robot wastes time it needs for the rest of the route.

  1. Run three times with the number as it is. Write balls out and hits each time.
  2. Change FEED_SECONDS by 0.2. Change nothing else.
  3. Run three more times. Keep the new number only if all the preloads leave on every run.
  4. Check the whole run still finishes before 28 seconds and ends where the route should end.

A hit on a wall target is practice. On a real hive, also write whether the hive tipped; that is the 20 points.

If shooting is not reliable by the end, that is a result too. Keep the dry run, or a route with no aimAndShoot() line, as the match plan and come back to shooting later.

Save your work

  • The speed table for this robot is in ShooterCalibration.java.
  • The worksheet shows balls out, hits and time for each run, including the bad ones.
  • The team has written down which autonomous it would use in a match today: shoot and park, park only, or leave.
  • The code builds. Commit it with a short message, for example “Meeting 12 auto shooting”.

Mentor preparation

  1. Shooting in autonomous needs four things: dry run switched off in INIT, FEED_ENABLED = true in StarterRobot.java, a speed table that covers the camera range at the shooting spot, and the tags printed for the chosen cell.
  2. Use the balls the robot is built for. The BIOBUZZ robot starts a match with four POLLEN.
  3. Set up the target: a real hive if you have access, otherwise the Meeting 5 tape wall.
  4. Leave the launcher’s P and F numbers alone today.
