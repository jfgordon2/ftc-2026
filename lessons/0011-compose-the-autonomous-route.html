Today’s goal

Leave with one autonomous route, chosen by the team, that ends in the right place three times in a row without shooting.

Meeting 5 built a practice path against a wall. Today the route is for the real field. First decide what is worth doing in 30 seconds, then measure the route, type it in as a list of steps, and run it empty.

Start here

  1. Decide what the autonomous is for. Choices and points.
  2. Plan the route on paper and on the floor. Planning steps.
  3. Type and run it empty. Run steps.
  4. Save. Save your work.

One robot runs at a time. Students who are not at the robot read the code for the next step, fill in predictions, or work on the build. Swap jobs after every test.

Before power

  • Take the balls out. Mark the route; nobody stands on it while the robot moves by itself.
  • One student drives. A different student keeps a hand near Stop on the Driver Station and may press it at any time without asking.
  • Press Stop and wait for everything to stop moving before you touch, load, measure or pick up the robot.
  • Clear a jam only with the robot switched off.
Stop first: if the robot goes the wrong way, something is loose, a ball jams, or the numbers on the screen look wrong, press Stop. Then work out why.

Steps and worksheets

Words you need

Leg: one straight drive or one turn. Route: the list of steps for one starting place and one target. Step: one line of the list. Dry run: the whole route with aiming but no shooting.

Rules that shape the route (manual rule G304): the robot starts on our side of the field, touching the field wall, not in the LOADING ZONE, not touching a FLOWER, inside the 18-inch starting size, with exactly four POLLEN. LEAVE and PARK are judged when AUTO ends.

Time is the catch. Our program stops itself at 28 seconds. Budget up to 4 seconds for each drive or turn, because each one must finish within 4 seconds, and about 12 seconds for an aim-and-shoot. A route of one drive, a shot, a turn and a drive fits. Add up your own route before you tape it.

Plan the route

  1. Open the field drawings in the manual (Figures 9-2, 9-3 and 11-1). Mark where the robot starts, where it shoots from, and where it ends.
  2. Break the route into legs. A leg is one straight drive or one turn. Fewer legs is better.
  3. Use your Meeting 10 speed: a drive must finish in 4 seconds, so no single drive can be longer than about 3 seconds’ worth of travel. Split longer drives into two legs, or ask the mentor about raising the drive power.
  4. Tape the route on the floor at full size. Our hive’s centre line is about 59 inches from our alliance wall, and the front of the upward cell is about 52 inches from the wall the robot starts on (our estimates from the manual’s drawings). The LOADING ZONE is 23 by 11 inches.
  5. Measure every leg with a tape measure and write it in the plan.

Type it in and run it empty

Routes live in Routes.java. A route is one list of steps, and the robot does them from top to bottom. The numbers here are examples:

public static final Step[] RED_AUDIENCE_A = {
        drive(8),
        aimAndShoot(),
        turn(-90),
        drive(30),
        turn(90),
        drive(30),
};
StepWhat the robot does
drive(inches)Drives that far. A negative number drives backward.
turn(degrees)Turns that much. Positive is right, negative is left.
aimAndShoot()Turns to face the tags, shoots if shooting is switched on, then turns back to the way it was facing. Leave this line out for a route that only parks.

There are eight lists: red or blue, audience-side or far-side cell, start A or B. Edit the one you chose in INIT. If you are not sure which cell will be facing up in a match, build and prove a route for each.

This program is the Meeting 9 program with a list. Open both files side by side: the fields, the checks in start(), and the MOVE, DONE and FAULT states are the same.

  1. Test each new leg by itself first with Tune Drive if it is 24 inches or 90 degrees, or with Autonomous One Action for other distances.
  2. Program: Autonomous Route. INIT, choose alliance and cell with the D-pad, press A for Start A, and check Shooting says DRY RUN. Check the route on the screen matches your list.
  3. Start. Watch which step the screen shows. When it stops, press Stop.
  4. Measure how far the robot ended from where it should be, and write the seconds.
  5. Change one number. Run again. Three runs in a row that end in the right place means the route is proven.
  6. At the stop before aimAndShoot(), read the camera range on the screen and write it down. Meeting 12 needs a speed table that covers it.

Save your work

  • The chosen route is in Routes.java and ran three times in a row to the right place.
  • The Meeting 9 one-action autonomous is still in the Driver Station list and still works.
  • The code builds. Commit it with a short message, for example “Meeting 11 auto route”.

Mentor preparation

  1. The autonomous programs stay still until MOUNTING_CONFIRMED (in Heading.java) and MOTION_ENABLED (in StarterRobot.java) are true. Do the Hub-direction and drive-direction checks from the Meeting 5 setup guide on the robot in use first.
  2. Decide where the route will be taped: a real field if you can get to one, otherwise open floor at least 8 by 8 feet. Bring painter’s tape and two tape measures.
  3. Print the tags for the cell the team will shoot at. Manual Figure 10-2 shows RED AUDIENCE and BLUE SCORING facing up at the start of a match (SCORING is the side away from the audience). Print the other cell’s tags too if the team wants a route for either.
  4. The 59-inch and 52-inch figures in the lesson are estimates from the manual’s drawings. Replace them with real measurements the first time the team is on a field.
  5. If drives are too slow to fit the time, DRIVE_POWER in Drive.java can be raised a little at a time. Re-run the Meeting 10 straight drive after any change.
